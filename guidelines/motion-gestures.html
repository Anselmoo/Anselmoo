01 The gestures

Nine gestures, and the gate that made them declare themselves

Nothing in this system moved until 2026-09-12, and --motion-gesture was deliberately null so it could not become a dead token. The portfolio surface moves, so the pair was filled in. Two rules came out of shipping them: every range uses the cover phase, which stays defined at any subject height, and a timeline-driven keyframe animates transform only — so no gesture can hide content. The demos below loop on a time clock so the card can show them standing still; on the real surface progress is the reader's scroll position and nothing plays on its own.

Sheet reveal

Transform only — 16px of travel, one spacing step, enough to read as arrival and not enough to look like a slide. The demo fades because it runs on a time clock; the real gesture does not touch opacity at all. cover 0% to cover 30%.

Seam draws outward

The cheapest gesture: a 1px rule scaling on one axis, composited. It lives only on .divider, which runs two of them from opposite origins so the seam draws outward from its glyph. A standalone drawn rule was tried three times and retired — see 03.

Thumbnail settles

Scale, never travel: a 64px square moved 16px reads as a jolt. It lands a beat after its row — cover 5% against the row's cover 0%.

Wordmark answers the scroll
Anselm

Weight and tracking, never position. Not a transition either — the scroll position is already continuous, so a transition on top would be a second animation lagging the first.

Read progress

Scale, not width: an animated width relayouts every frame and the generator fails the build for it. No percentage readout — scroll depth is neither reading progress nor time remaining.

Index rows stagger

Each row carries its own timeline, so the stagger is a consequence of position rather than a list of delays. Inserting a row renumbers nothing.

Mark draws itself

The cover mark on a dash offset: the Gaussian draws and the axis is there from the first frame, because a peak against nothing is not yet a measurement and an A with no bar is not yet a letter. stroke-dashoffset is paint, so this is the guard-free class.

Cue retires
eight sheets

The one place opacity is animated on a scroll timeline, and it is legal because it fades out: a cue still on screen after the reader has taken it is no longer a cue. Failure leaves content visible, which is the direction that costs nothing.

Letters rise
Anselm

The cover entrance, 60ms apart. Travel is in em and not px, so a 62px display letter and a 40px one rise by the same proportion of themselves — in px the responsive pair would read as two different animations.

360 is 3 × --motion-fast exactly, and the multiple is checked rather than asserted. An arbitrary number beside a 120ms token reads as two motion systems on one page; a whole multiple reads as one system at two speeds. Three is the smallest multiple that reads as a travel rather than a switch.

03 Two faults the gestures found
A separator may not depend on scroll to exist

The same fault as the reveal, one property along. A scaleX-from-zero gesture needs its range to complete, and both ends of a document make that impossible: an element at the top never scrolls into its cover range — measured at 41 % on first paint and staying there, a hairline drawn 41 % across — while one at the bottom never scrolls past it, measured at scaleX(0) at the very end of the document, so the footer separator was simply absent.

"Use it below the fold" was the first patch and it is not a rule anyone can follow: the safe zone is the middle of a document, which no component can know it is in. Retired for standalone rules. The gesture keeps one home, .divider, where the element is the gesture and the glyph and label carry the seam whether or not it has drawn.

A gesture may not own the content

The first reveal-in animated opacity from 0 with fill: both. All seven sheets were invisible at every scroll position — including sheets scrolled far past the viewport. Measured: animation attached, playState running, ViewTimeline live, currentTime -5.9%. Negative progress with both pins an element to its from keyframe, so opacity: 0 was forever. No console error.

Two faults, one symptom. The entry phase is ill-defined for a subject taller than the scrollport, and the sheets run to 2662px; and opacity had no business in a timeline-driven keyframe at all. Both are fixed: every range is now cover, and the guarded class animates transform only. A broken timeline now costs a 16px offset instead of the page.
