01Series palette

Three greys, and a pattern for each

There is no colour in this system, the imagery layer included. The hero, spots, banner, ornament, thumbnails and both seed motifs use three grey series one 0.155 step of OKLCH lightness apart, at chroma 0. Lightness alone is a weak channel at chart size, so every series also carries a pattern: the encoding is redundant rather than resting on one channel. Solved by _scripts/build-imagery.mjs, recorded in imagery/series.json.

03A pattern is geometry, ground and line

Grey is not a colour here, so the tone of a pattern's ground and of its lines are free choices alongside its geometry. Grey on white, grey on black and ink on grey read as different fills — more than three distinct fills from three geometries, without inventing a fourth. The hero uses the alternation: solid ink, a grey hatch on paper, a grey cross-hatch on ink, so every cut edge is light against dark.

04Why the pattern is not decoration
Lightness alone, at chart width

Three greys 0.155 apart are separable as 72px swatches and genuinely hard at 9px of bar. Photocopy it, or read it at a projector's gamma, and two of the three merge. The desaturation floor checks whether two values are distinguishable, not whether they are distinguishable at the size the data is actually drawn.

Lightness and pattern

Redundant encoding, the rule this system already states for colour — “never category by one channel alone”. The pattern survives desaturation, photocopying and a 9px bar, because its distinguishing feature is geometry and not value. A tile is opaque — series grey ground, series-1 lines — so a papercut layer never shows the layer beneath it.

The patterns come off the 4px division like everything else: a 4px period at 45° for the hatch, a 5px grid for the cross-hatch, both at hairline width. Three is the cap — solid, hatch, cross-hatch — and a fourth would need a fourth geometry, not a fourth angle: hatches at 45° and 135° read as the same texture at nine pixels. The hue-bearing series this card used to show is retired; nothing in the system may reach for it.
